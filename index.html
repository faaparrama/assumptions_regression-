📚 What are the regression assumptions? (LINE — click to expand)

OLS regression relies on four key assumptions, remembered as LINE. Violating them doesn't always break your model, but it affects which conclusions you can trust.

L
Linearity
The mean of Y changes linearly with X. A curved residual pattern means you're fitting a line to a curve.
I
Independence
Errors are not correlated with each other. Most critical for time-ordered or grouped data.
N
Normality
Residuals are approximately normally distributed. Needed for valid p-values and CIs, especially with small samples.
E
Equal Variance
Variance of residuals is constant across all fitted values (homoscedasticity). A funnel shape violates this.

🎛️ Model Parameters
📊 Predictors (p) 1
1 = simple, 2–4 = multiple regression
Multiple regression: Adding predictors shows how R² grows (sometimes spuriously). The Adj. R² penalizes for extra variables. Notice: residual diagnostics remain the same regardless of how many predictors you use — that's the key insight!
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Sample Size 30
more points = clearer pattern
Why it matters: Larger samples make patterns more visible and give more reliable estimates. With small n, random noise can hide true relationships or create false ones.
📌 Intercept 0.0
where line crosses Y-axis
The intercept (b₀): The predicted Y when X = 0. Shifts the line up or down without affecting residual patterns.
📐 Slope 0.50
positive ↗ / negative ↘
The slope (b₁): For each 1-unit increase in X, Y changes by b₁. Notice how R² increases with steeper slopes relative to noise.
🌫️ Noise (SD) 1.0
scatter around the line
Error variance (σ²): More noise = lower R², wider CIs, harder-to-detect effects. This is the irreducible error.
⚠️ Assumption Violations
💥 Outlier 0.0
high-leverage point pushed up/down
High-leverage outlier: Places an extreme point at the highest X value — this is a high-leverage point, meaning it has outsized influence on the slope. Not all outliers are high-leverage (an extreme Y at average X pulls less). In practice, check Cook's Distance to measure how much each point shifts the regression line.
🌀 Heteroscedasticity 0.00
error grows with X (megaphone)
Constant variance violated. OLS assumes equal variance at all X levels. A "megaphone" in residuals means SEs are unreliable — use robust SE or WLS.
〰️ Non-Linearity 0.00
bend (quadratic term)
Linearity violated. A U-shaped residual pattern means you're fitting a line to curved data. Add polynomial terms or transform.
🔗 Autocorrelation 0.00
errors correlated in sequence (AR1)
Independence violated. This matters most when data are collected in sequence (time series, repeated measures). Look for "runs" of positive or negative residuals in the order plot. The Durbin-Watson test (range 0–4, ideal near 2) formally tests for AR(1) autocorrelation. Fix: use GLS, ARIMA, or cluster-robust standard errors.
